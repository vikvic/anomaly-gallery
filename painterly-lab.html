Ordering. Landmarks and blob targets are computed on the unmodified source and cached, then reused for both panel 2 and panel 4. MediaPipe was trained on photographs, so it should never be handed a stylised image — and the coordinates are geometric, so the pass does not invalidate them.

Shade spread is the live range of 1 + (hostLum/hostMean − 1) × 0.45 across the graft footprint — the exact multiply inside graftFeature that makes a graft sit in a surface rather than on it. A spread near zero means flat lighting has degenerated it to a no-op.

What the measurements said. Across 29 vendored still lifes, at their own live targets and real graft sizes, that spread runs 0.28–1.13 with a median of 0.60. A synthetic flat photo sits at 0.03. Three results follow, and the first two were not what I expected:
 · Global chiaroscuro barely helps. A 50px footprint is 8% of a 620px canvas, so a whole-image gradient hardly varies across it — 0.063 → 0.068 at full strength.
 · Kuwahara actively hurts — 0.063 → 0.023. The stage that most makes a photo look painted is the stage that most starves the graft. That tension is real; the slider exposes it rather than hiding it.
 · Object-scale form shading is what works — 0.34 on its own. Real paintings have modelled form inside the blob, not just a lit room; shading the target like the hemisphere findBlobs already assumes it is restores the thing the op was calibrated on.
