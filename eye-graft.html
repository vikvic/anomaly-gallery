Eye Graft

An eye, taken from a real painted portrait and grafted onto fruit in a still life. Nothing is generated — the eye is oil paint harvested from another canvas of the same period, so it arrives already carrying the right brushwork, palette and varnish.

1 · Harvested eyes

Face landmarks give a 16-point contour ring per eye. The cut is expanded well past the ring so the lid and socket shading come too — a bare eyeball reads as a sticker; the socket is what makes it read as anatomy. Click one to graft it.

2 · Target

The graft point is found with a distance transform over a mask of warm saturated paint. Its maximum is by definition the pixel furthest from any edge — the centre of the fruit — and the distance value there is the largest circle that fits inside it. A colour centroid does not work: it lands on the boundary and the eye straddles two objects.

3 · Graft

Three things make it sit in the surface rather than on it: a spherical warp so the eye curves with the fruit; a multiply of the host's own luminance gradient so the apple's light and shadow run across the eye; and a soft crease ring where lid meets flesh.

Sources: Wikimedia Commons, public domain. Face landmarks: MediaPipe FaceLandmarker (478 points), loaded from a CDN. Everything else is plain canvas pixel work — no generation, no inference beyond locating the eyes.
